There's something appealing about the "digital garden" metaphor for personal websites. Unlike blogs, which present ideas as finished products arranged chronologically, gardens emphasize growth, connection, and ongoing cultivation.

A blog post says: "Here is my complete thought on X."

A digital garden says: "Here's what I'm thinking about X right now. It might change."

The garden model feels more honest about how ideas actually develop—through revision, connection, and time. Not every thought needs to be polished into a final form. Some can remain seedlings, waiting to be tended.
