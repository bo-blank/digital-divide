A friend mentioned she'd been feeling anxious and couldn't figure out why. Nothing specific had happened. Work was fine. Relationships were fine. Just this low hum of dread that wouldn't go away.

Then she checked her screen time. Seven hours of social media the day before. Not unusual for her—that was the problem.

We don't connect the scroll to the feeling. The dopamine hits come instantly; the costs accumulate slowly. An hour on Instagram doesn't feel like anything in particular. A year of hours feels like something's wrong with you that you can't name.

The research is pretty clear at this point: heavy social media use correlates with anxiety, depression, and decreased life satisfaction, particularly in young people. But "correlates" lets us off the hook. We can tell ourselves we're different, that we handle it fine, that it's just correlation not causation.

Maybe. But also: when was the last time you put your phone down and felt *better*? When was the last time an hour of scrolling left you energized, inspired, connected in a way that lasted beyond the session?

I'm not saying delete everything. I'm saying pay attention. The depth of a scroll has a cost, and the invoice comes due in currency we don't always recognize.
